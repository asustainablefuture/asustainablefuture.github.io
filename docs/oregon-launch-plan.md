# A Sustainable Future: Oregon Building Support Launch Plan

Verified against official sources on August 8, 2026. This is an operating and marketing plan, not legal advice.

## Executive decision

A Sustainable Future should launch as a free, independent **readiness and navigation service** for Oregon building owners. Its first job is to help an owner answer four questions: Is this building likely covered? What is due and when? What data is missing? Which work can the owner do now, and which work requires an Oregon-qualified professional?

The organization should not position itself as ODOE, a regulator, an energy auditor, an engineer, or a compliance certifier. ODOE expressly warns building owners that third-party solicitations can look official and that the agency does not endorse individual businesses. Every outreach, intake, and website page should say that A Sustainable Future is independent and that official instructions control.

## What the law currently requires

Oregon's Building Performance Standard is in [ORS 469.275–469.291](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors469.html) and [OAR chapter 330, division 300](https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/displayDivisionRules.action?selectedDivision=7304). It is based on ASHRAE Standard 100 with Oregon amendments. ODOE's [program page](https://www.oregon.gov/energy/save-energy/Pages/BPS.aspx), [compliance guide](https://www.oregon.gov/energy/save-energy/Documents/BPS001-compliance.pdf), and [FAQ](https://www.oregon.gov/energy/save-energy/Documents/BPS-FAQs.pdf) are the primary operational references.

### Tier 1

Tier 1 generally covers nonresidential buildings, hotels, and motels with at least 35,000 square feet of gross floor area, excluding parking-garage area. Owners must benchmark energy use, calculate the applicable energy-use-intensity target, maintain an operations and maintenance program and an energy-management plan, and meet the target or use a statutory compliance path. Certain work and forms require a Qualified Person, Qualified Energy Manager, or Qualified Energy Auditor.

The first compliance dates are:

| Gross floor area | First Tier 1 deadline |
| --- | --- |
| 200,000 square feet or more | June 1, 2028 |
| 90,000 to under 200,000 square feet | June 1, 2029 |
| 35,000 to under 90,000 square feet | June 1, 2030 |

The statute provides for a warning process and civil penalties. ODOE's current [penalties and incentives guide](https://www.oregon.gov/energy/save-energy/Documents/BPS010-Incentives-penalties.pdf) explains that failure to report can lead to an initial penalty of up to $5,000 plus up to $1 per square foot per year; a building that reports but does not perform can face up to $1,500 plus up to $0.20 per square foot per year. Owners should verify building-specific exposure with ODOE or counsel.

### Tier 2

Tier 2 generally covers nonresidential buildings, hotels, and motels from 20,000 to under 35,000 square feet, plus specified uses of at least 35,000 square feet: multifamily housing, hospitals, schools, universities, dormitories, barracks, prisons, and senior or residential care facilities. The first report is due July 1, 2028 and repeats every five years.

Current law requires Tier 2 reporting of energy-use intensity and the applicable target. It does **not currently require Tier 2 buildings to meet that target**, and ODOE guidance says there are currently no Tier 2 penalties. Future legislation or rulemaking could change that; the operating system must record the source and verification date for every compliance statement.

### Portland overlap

Portland's separate program generally requires commercial buildings of 20,000 square feet or more to report annual energy performance by April 22. The city's [official reporting page](https://www.portland.gov/bps/article/542355) says roughly 1,000 buildings are subject to that program. Coverage and exemptions differ from the state standard, so an owner may have two distinct reporting duties. The city's [2024 results](https://www.portland.gov/bps/climate-action/energy-reporting/annual-data-and-results) provide an immediate public pilot dataset: 1,039 expected reports, 822 received, and 79% reported compliance. Treat the 217-report difference as a prospecting signal, not proof that 217 current owners are in violation.

## Market sizing and priority

ODOE's [2026 annual report](https://www.oregon.gov/energy/Data-and-Reports/Documents/2026-Building-Performance-Standard-Annual-Report.pdf) says the agency mailed more than 30,000 pieces in June 2025 to buildings and owners identified through county assessor data and assigned Unique Building Identifiers. That is a defensible **upper-funnel signal**, not a count of definitely covered buildings or owners. Mixed use, exemptions, duplicate owner records, and assessor-data quality make a single exact statewide market count misleading.

Prioritize by urgency and ability to help:

1. Tier 1 buildings at or above 200,000 square feet, due June 1, 2028.
2. Tier 2 buildings due July 1, 2028, especially organizations with portfolio-wide data gaps.
3. Portland buildings with a public reporting gap or incomplete benchmarking record.
4. Tier 1 buildings from 90,000 to under 200,000 square feet, due in 2029.
5. Tier 1 buildings from 35,000 to under 90,000 square feet, due in 2030.

Within each group, give additional weight to buildings likely above target, owners with multiple properties, underserved/rural communities, public-interest uses, and owners eligible for live incentives. Never claim coverage or noncompliance from a list alone.

## Service design

Offer six bounded, auditable services at no cost:

1. **Coverage screen:** map building type and floor area to a likely tier, Portland overlap, and questions for ODOE.
2. **Data-readiness map:** inventory meters, fuels, property uses, gross floor area, occupancy, tenants, account holders, and missing months.
3. **Benchmarking setup:** organize records for ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager and Oregon's BEAM portal without submitting or certifying information outside the owner's authorization.
4. **Utility-data navigation:** help request whole-building data. Oregon's rules require qualifying large utilities to provide up to 60 months of aggregate data at no cost for covered buildings meeting account thresholds—generally at least three unique nonresidential accounts or five residential accounts—and to support direct Portfolio Manager-compatible exchange.
5. **Incentive triage:** check the live ODOE and utility pages, record the verification date, and identify eligibility questions. ODOE currently lists about $11.6 million available in BERI Round 2 across Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tribal allocations, open through December 11, 2026 or until funds are exhausted. Balances and eligibility can change.
6. **Qualified handoff:** produce a clean package for an approved auditor or other qualified professional when the law reserves work for that credential.

The intake should collect only what is needed: contact role, organization, building address, approximate square feet, primary use, notice/UBID if available, utility providers, help requested, consent to contact, and source. Store uploaded notices and bills in restricted storage; never put them in the public website repository.

## Ethical owner-contact pipeline

Build a reproducible, source-aware pipeline instead of paying people to search randomly:

`building record → legal owner entity → organization contact route → role or decision maker → outreach permission/status → service outcome`

Permitted starting sources include the Portland public building spreadsheet, county assessor records, Oregon Secretary of State business records, public agency/property directories, the owner's official website, and owner-provided information. For every contact, store the source URL or dataset, retrieval date, owner/building match confidence, and whether the address or number is a business role or a personal contact.

Use automation for deterministic joins and deduplication; use trained humans for edge-case review and quality assurance. Do not scrape private BEAM records, evade access controls, infer personal emails, buy opaque consumer-contact lists, or use pretexts. Honor removal and correction requests immediately.

Ask ODOE for a transparent partnership or referral process. The ideal arrangement is that ODOE includes a neutral list of independent assistance resources or an owner opt-in in its normal communications. A Sustainable Future may ask for legally releasable inventory fields through a public-records request—such as UBID, address, square footage, building type, likely tier, public owner name, and notice status—but should not ask the agency to disclose personal email addresses unless disclosure and use are explicitly lawful. No outreach should imply that ODOE supplied or endorsed a lead.

## Outreach strategy

The core message is: “We are an independent nonprofit initiative offering free administrative and data-readiness help. We do not represent ODOE, and official requirements control.” The first message should name the public source used, state why the building may be relevant without declaring it covered, link to the official ODOE page, explain the bounded free service, and include a one-step opt-out.

Use four trigger windows:

- Within days of a public ODOE program or incentive update.
- After an owner opts in through ODOE, a utility, a professional association, or the website.
- Six to twelve months before a known reporting milestone.
- Before Portland's April 22 annual deadline.

Notification-based timing is acceptable only when the notice date is public, owner-provided, or shared under an explicit, lawful agency agreement. Do not monitor private accounts or use notification timing in a way that suggests agency affiliation.

Channels in priority order:

1. Trusted referrals from ODOE, utilities, cities, counties, trade associations, and qualified professionals.
2. Targeted email to public role-based or clearly business-purpose addresses.
3. Small owner clinics for portfolios, property managers, nonprofits, schools, and local governments.
4. Carefully controlled manual calling for urgent, high-value buildings whose public business contact route is incomplete.

### Cold-calling test

Cold calling is a test, not the default program. Run a randomized pilot of 100–150 likely relevant organizations: half receive the email sequence only; half receive the same sequence plus no more than two manual calls to a publicly listed business number. Measure decision-maker connection, qualified intake, benchmarking completion, opt-outs, complaints, staff minutes, and energy opportunity. Stop the call arm if complaints exceed 1%, opt-outs exceed 5%, or qualified-intake lift does not justify the additional time.

Before launch, have Oregon counsel review the call script, entity status, and process. The federal Telemarketing Sales Rule generally exempts most true business-to-business calls, but 2024 amendments prohibit deceptive or misleading statements in B2B telemarketing. Oregon's current [ORS 646A.370–646A.376](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors646a.html) now covers calls and texts made with automated dialing/announcing devices, prohibits identity and purpose misrepresentation, restricts automated calls to 8 a.m.–8 p.m., and limits them to three in 24 hours. Use manual dialing only, no prerecorded or synthesized voice, no automated texts, no consumer wireless numbers, accurate caller ID, honest identification and purpose, and an internal do-not-contact list. A nonprofit status or free offer should never be treated as automatic exemption from every rule.

## Staffing and governance

For the first 90 days:

- Program lead: 0.5–1.0 FTE for partnerships, owner intake, service quality, and escalation.
- Data/operations lead: 0.5 FTE for source ingestion, identity resolution, CRM controls, reporting, and audits.
- Volunteer or student coordinator: 0.25–0.5 FTE for training, scheduling, and quality review.
- Contract/referral bench: Oregon-qualified energy auditors, qualified persons, legal counsel, and privacy/security support as needed.

Students may assist with documented research and owner support under supervision, but should not make compliance determinations, handle uncontrolled sensitive documents, or perform credential-reserved work. Every building record should have an accountable staff owner and a second-person review before an external compliance claim is sent.

## Measurement

The north-star metric is **verified annual site energy reduced per dollar and per staff/volunteer hour**, with reporting completion as a leading indicator.

Track the full funnel:

- unique buildings sourced, deduplicated, and quality-reviewed;
- likely tier and source confidence;
- contactability, delivery, bounce, opt-out, and complaint rates;
- owner responses, qualified intakes, and scheduled readiness sessions;
- Portfolio Manager setup or correction, complete 12-month datasets, and BEAM submissions;
- qualified-professional referrals and completed audits;
- incentive applications and awards;
- measures implemented, project cost, utility savings, kWh/therms/MMBtu reduced, and greenhouse-gas impact where defensible;
- staff minutes and total cost per sourced, engaged, reporting-ready, and energy-improved building.

Never count an opened email as impact. Energy savings should be utility-verified where possible or clearly labeled with the estimation method, baseline, weather normalization, and uncertainty.

## First 90 days

### Days 1–30: establish trust and validate data

- Configure authenticated domain email with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC after the user approves any required subscription.
- Publish the Oregon-focused website and plain-language guide.
- Adopt independence, privacy, records-retention, correction, and do-not-contact policies.
- Import and deduplicate the Portland public dataset; manually audit a random sample of 50 records.
- Seek meetings with ODOE, Portland, Energy Trust of Oregon, large utilities, BOMA Oregon, school and local-government networks, and qualified-professional associations.
- Recruit at least three qualified professionals willing to accept clearly scoped referrals.

Gate to continue: at least 90% building-to-owner match accuracy in the audited sample, zero unresolved high-risk privacy issues, and partner feedback that the service does not look governmental.

### Days 31–60: run the service pilot

- Contact a small, high-confidence Portland cohort using the email-first protocol.
- Run two virtual clinics and 15–25 one-to-one readiness sessions.
- Complete the controlled calling experiment only after counsel review.
- Test three message frames: free readiness help, deadline clarity, and current incentive access.
- Produce owner-facing readiness maps and document every handoff boundary.

Gate to continue: at least 10% qualified response from high-confidence contacts, at least 15 completed readiness maps, bounce below 5%, complaints below 1%, and no false affiliation incidents.

### Days 61–90: prove outcomes and choose scale

- Follow the first cohort through benchmarking, BEAM, audit, incentive, or project milestones.
- Compare outreach channels by qualified intake and staff time, not opens.
- Publish an anonymized dashboard with funnel, service, and energy metrics.
- Submit a precise public-records request or partnership proposal informed by pilot data.
- Decide whether to expand statewide, specialize in one owner segment, or pause acquisition and improve delivery capacity.

Gate to scale: at least 25 reporting-ready buildings or a documented equivalent milestone, at least five qualified-professional/incentive handoffs, owners rating the service useful, and a credible method for measuring energy outcomes.

## Risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Appearing to be ODOE or a government contractor | Repeated independent-service disclosure; official links; partner and counsel review; never imitate notices. |
| Wrong coverage or deadline claim | “Likely” language; source/version dates; second-person review; ODOE referral for determinations. |
| Sensitive utility or tenant data exposure | Data minimization, restricted storage, least privilege, retention schedule, incident plan; no uploads in the website repository. |
| Poor contact data or harassment | Source log, confidence score, low frequency, role-based routes, immediate suppression/correction. |
| Unqualified technical work | Written scope boundaries; credential check; qualified-professional handoff. |
| Incentive money changes or runs out | Live-link verification on every case; never promise funding. |
| Outreach volume exceeds service capacity | Cohort caps, intake queue, published response times, stop/go gates. |
| Vanity metrics replace energy impact | Tie every campaign to reporting readiness, implemented measures, and normalized savings. |

## Immediate decisions

1. Approve the independent readiness-service position and no-affiliation language.
2. Approve a 90-day Portland-first pilot rather than statewide mass outreach.
3. Approve only the minimum email subscription required for two accountable users, using aliases or groups for shared addresses where the provider permits it.
4. Name a program lead and data steward before importing contact information.
5. Seek partner and counsel review before any cold-call campaign.
